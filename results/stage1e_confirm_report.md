## 4. Decoding robustness (BINDING)

Frozen rule (verbatim, Addendum A section B):

> **DECODING ROBUSTNESS (binding):** every confirmatory contrast must hold
> in direction under argmax decoding of the same distributions. All lifts
> are reported under both decodings, always beside both arms' raw MAEs.
> Rationale: EV decoding shrinks variance and can inflate lift by damaging
> the hedging baseline (results/rescore_ev_vs_argmax.md).

And Addendum A section E rule 1:

> Every reported lift appears beside both arms' raw MAEs, under both
> decodings (extends Amendment A8).

Why this rule exists, in one paragraph: expected-value decoding averages the
twin's stated distribution, which squeezes its predictions toward the middle of
the scale. That helps an over-confident arm and hurts a hedging one. The
demographics-only baseline hedges, so EV decoding damages it and can inflate any
lift measured against it. On the Stage 1 development runs, argmax beat EV on the
baseline arm in every run, and one diagnostic's lift changed sign
(`results/rescore_ev_vs_argmax.md`, `memory/metric-decoding-caveat.md`). Both
decodings are computed from the same stored distributions, so this is a scoring
question, not a second experiment, and it costs no extra model calls.
